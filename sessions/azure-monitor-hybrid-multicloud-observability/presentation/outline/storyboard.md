# Whole-Service Observability storyboard

Review date: 2026-10-01 (rewrite). This is the authoritative teaching contract for both Observability decks: the decks take their slide order, IDs and titles from this table. Notes live in `presentation/dual-notes.json`; demos in `demos/demo-content.json`.

**Audience and level (presenter decision, 2026-10-01):** IT Pros and cloud operators who are here to learn what monitoring a hybrid cloud and observability actually are. The session is built on a real team meeting. The presenter's team runs the lab environments, some of them treated as production. A corporate monitoring solution is shared with lab environments other teams manage, so the team is flooded with alerts for things it does not own. In the meeting someone proposed another monitoring solution just for the team's own labs, and the thinking stayed on-premises, across the two datacenters. The presenter's answer: we need more than monitoring; we need observability; and the labs that extend into Azure and AWS must be in the same single pane of glass from the start. Every slide has to pass one test: would the presenter say this in that meeting? Configuration detail (data collection rules, associations, role names, rollup settings, query guards) lives in the speaker notes and the field manual, not on the slides. Each idea is said once.

**Application (presenter decision, 2026-09-26):** the session's service is **IIC Hybrid Orders**, the same application as the Hybrid Operations session, deployed across Azure, the on-premises private cloud and AWS. Its design of record is `lab/design/HYBRID-APPLICATION-HEALTH-DESIGN.md`.

**Deployment state:** the deck presents the environment as fully deployed and configured. Slides do not track lab build status; that lives in `lab/status/`.

**Central question:** Can customers complete an order right now, and if not, why not, when the parts of the service run in Azure, AWS and the on-premises private cloud?
